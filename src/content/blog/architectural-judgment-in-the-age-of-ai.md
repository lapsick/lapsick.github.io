AI coding assistants can now produce working code faster than most of us can type, but writing
code was never the hard part of building software. The harder questions are the architectural
ones: how to divide a system into parts, where to draw boundaries, which trade-offs to accept, and
how a decision made today will hold up under load, change, and time.

These are the judgments an AI can suggest but not own — they belong to the engineer who has to
live with the results.

If you want to sharpen that judgment, I'd recommend
[*Fundamentals of Software Architecture*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/fundamentals-of-software/9781098175504/)
by Mark Richards and Neal Ford. It treats architecture as a set of deliberate decisions and
trade-offs rather than a fixed set of rules — which is exactly the mindset that stays relevant
regardless of how good the tooling gets.

As agents take over more of the typing, the people who understand *why* a system is shaped the
way it is will be the ones steering the work.
